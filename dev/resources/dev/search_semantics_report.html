Search engine comparison

Where Metabase's search engines return different cards for the same query, and what each needs typed to find what a user wants.
The examples are hand-picked to show how the engines behave, so they say little about production traffic. They compare which cards come back and ignore their order, which leaves ranking out entirely.

    Query matching rules

    BehaviorIn-placeappdb/H2appdb/PostgreSQLSemantic vector
    Searches Live entity columns Materialized appdb text Indexed appdb lexemes Document embeddings
    Multiple words Any query word can suffice. Every query word must match. Every processed term must match, unless the query uses operators. Compares the query's overall meaning; individual words need not match.
    Partial words Any substring, so rate matches Moderated. Any substring, including inside longer words. Prefix of the final eligible word only; no inner substring. No defined prefix or substring rule.
    Operators Implicit OR across words; or, -, and quotes are literal text. Implicit AND across words; or, -, and quotes are literal text. Conjunction, disjunction, negation, and phrase matching. Reads the query in context, with no guaranteed Boolean or phrase operations.
    Language No stopword removal or stemming. No stopword removal or stemming. Stopwords and stemming follow the site locale or search-language setting. Stopwords and stemming are model-dependent.

    Semantic search unions these vector hits with keyword hits, then ranks the combined results. For every case here, the keyword hits are the appdb/PostgreSQL hits. When it finds fewer results than its threshold (100 by default), as it always does in a small catalog, it also appends appdb's results, as long as its own arms found something. This page scores semantic search twice: Semantic only is the vector arm alone, and Semantic with fallback is what it returns, modeling appdb as PostgreSQL.

    Scores by goal

    Each cell is the F1 score against the goal's correct cards, from 0 (none right) to 1 (exactly right).

    How to read the table

    • F1 combines precision, the share of returned cards that are correct, and recall, the share of correct cards that come back. It is their harmonic mean, so it is high only when both are high. Returning one of three correct cards and nothing else scores 0.5.
    • 0.40 → 1.00 means 0.40 for the goal's original query and 1.00 for the engine's best query. One number means the best query scores the same.
    • A best query uses words a user could think of for the goal without knowing which cards exist, and any syntax the engine supports.
    • A bold score with a blue bar is the best, or tied best, for that goal.
    • Hover a cell for precision and recall. Click a goal to open its case.
